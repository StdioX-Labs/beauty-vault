# Are Lash Extensions Safe? The Complete Safety and Aftercare Guide

## The Short Answer

Lash extensions are safe for most people when they are applied by a trained technician in a clean setup, with the right adhesive and the right weight for your natural lashes. Problems almost always come from the same four places: dirty tools, the wrong glue, lashes that are too heavy, and poor aftercare. This guide shows you how to avoid each one.

## What Can Go Wrong (and Why)

### 1. Allergic or irritant reactions

Lash adhesive can irritate the eyelids, especially while it is still curing. Signs include redness, itching, swelling, watery eyes and a burning feeling. A reaction can come from the adhesive, the primer or the tape, or from simple irritation caused by fumes.

* **What helps:** tell your technician about any past reactions, ask for a patch test if you have sensitive skin, and ask what adhesive is used.
* **UV lashes:** the adhesive cures under a UV lamp in seconds, so there are no lingering fumes while you lie there. Many clients with sensitive eyes find them more comfortable. This does not mean no one can ever react, so the patch test advice still applies.
* **If a reaction starts:** book a removal, do not pick at the lashes, and see a doctor if swelling or pain does not settle quickly.

### 2. Infection

Unclean tools, unwashed hands or sharing disposable items can carry bacteria to the eye area. Eye infections are not something to ignore.

* **What helps:** your technician should wash their hands, use clean or single-use items on every client, and keep tweezers sanitised between clients. Feel free to ask how they do it. A good salon is happy to show you.
* **Aftercare also matters:** lashes that are never cleaned collect oil, makeup and dead skin along the lash line.

### 3. Damage to your natural lashes

One extension is meant to sit on one natural lash. Damage happens when the extension is too heavy or too long for that lash, when several lashes are stuck together, or when extensions are applied too close to the skin.

* **What helps:** a technician who maps your lashes first and chooses the length, curl and thickness for *your* lashes, not just the style you saw online.
* **Rest and refill:** keep your set full with refills about every 2 to 3 weeks, rather than letting it thin out and then starting again heavy.

### 4. Pulling and bad removal

Picking, pulling or peeling off extensions can take your natural lashes with them. Removal should be done with a professional remover, not by hand.

* **What helps:** book a removal (KES 1,160 at our branches).

## How to Choose a Safe Lash Tech in Nairobi

Ask these before you book:

1. Are you trained, and in which method (regular, UV, volume)?
2. What adhesive do you use, and can I do a patch test?
3. How do you sanitise your tools between clients?
4. Will you map my lashes and choose the weight for them?
5. Do you offer refills and removal, and at what price?

Be careful with very cheap sets, rushed appointments, no questions about your eyes, or a technician who will not explain what they use. Low prices often mean skipped steps or poor materials. Our prices include VAT and are listed openly on the [lash menu](/lashes).

## Aftercare: What to Do and When

### The first 24 to 48 hours

* **Regular lashes:** keep them dry for 24 to 48 hours. The adhesive is still finishing its cure. Avoid steam, saunas, hot showers on your face and heavy sweating.
* **UV lashes:** the adhesive is fully cured when you leave, so you can wash your face the same day. Still be gentle.

### Every day

* **Cleanse the lashes.** Use a lash-safe foam cleanser and a soft brush once a day, working from the base outward, then rinse and pat dry. Clean lashes last longer and keep the eyelid healthy.
* **Brush them.** Use a clean spoolie each morning to keep them neat.
* **Do not rub, pull or pick.** Pat your face dry, do not rub it.
* **Skip oil near the eyes.** Oil-based makeup removers, creams and mascara can break down the bond. Choose oil-free products.
* **Skip waterproof mascara** and heated lash curlers on extensions.
* **Sleep on your back** or side if you can, to avoid crushing the lashes.

### Every 2 to 3 weeks

* **Book a refill.** Refills are cheaper than a full set. At our branches a classic refill is KES 2,320 (UV KES 2,900), and refills of volume sets are priced on the [lash menu](/lashes). Refill within 21 days of your last appointment for the best price and the best look.

### When to see a professional

Book a removal and see a doctor if you have swelling, strong or lasting pain, discharge, blurred vision or redness that gets worse. Do not wait it out, and do not try to remove a whole set yourself.

## UV Lashes vs Regular Lashes: Does Safety Differ?

The extensions themselves are the same. The difference is the adhesive and how it sets. UV adhesive cures under a lamp in seconds, which means no lingering fumes and a simpler first-day routine. Regular adhesive air-dries over 24 to 48 hours. Both need a trained technician, clean tools and good aftercare. Read more in [UV Lashes vs Regular Lashes](/uv-lashes-vs-regular-lashes/) and the [Complete Guide to UV Lashes in Kenya](/uv-lashes-in-kenya-complete-guide/).
